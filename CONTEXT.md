## External exchange

Workspaces can send **external exchanges** to people outside TrialAgents ([scope](docs/product/EXTERNAL_EXCHANGE.md); migration 064, `src/domain/external-exchange.ts`, `src/app-services/exchange-email.ts`, `src/server/external-exchange.ts`, recipient page `/exchange#<token>`). An app creates an exchange **group** (brief, shared documents, response form, requested document slots, confidentiality gate, per-type email defaults) and one **exchange per recipient**, bound to an app record, each with its own unguessable link, optional email verification by one-time code (on by default when documents are shared), a 1–90 day deadline that owners can extend, and revocation that keeps everything received. Recipients save drafts and resubmit until the deadline (versioned, time-stamped; up to 10 files of 25 MB), upload requested documents into named slots (requested / received / accepted), download logged documents, and use a private thread; the workspace can publish a question as an anonymised clarification ("A recipient asked") or an addendum to the whole group. Gates: none, click-through acknowledgement (name, email, time logged) or signed document required (other documents unlock when the owner accepts the signed upload). After the deadline the page is read-only; messages stay open unless the group says otherwise.

Preparation that nobody outside has seen yet (group, links, and documents, brief or slots before the first email or visit) is applied on a person's own click in an app and logged; since template guidance 1.3.24 (13 November 2026) one review is shown when something goes out or comes in, and the app's record changes in that action commit in the same transaction. Every workspace-side write is a reviewed workspace operation (`create_exchange_group`, `invite_exchange`, `update_exchange_brief`, `share_exchange_documents`, `request_exchange_document`, `extend_exchange`, `revoke_exchange`, `accept_exchange_submission`, `accept_exchange_file`, `reply_exchange`, `publish_exchange_clarification`, `save_exchange_email_default`, `send_exchange_email`); sends, publications, replies and acceptances are confirmed by a person, never by an assistant. Outbound email goes from `no-reply@trialagents.com` with display name "<name> at TrialAgents" (organization, first name or custom name part; the suffix is fixed; the last choice is remembered per user and workspace) and Reply-To the sending person; emails carry links only, no attachments and no tracking pixels; the footer names the sender and organization and links to report abuse. Default subject/body per type (invitation, reminder, clarification, message, extended, revoked) with placeholders; an app's defaults replace the platform's and a saved workspace default replaces the app's. `prepare_exchange_email` renders the batch exactly as it will be sent (batch text, per-recipient personalisation, recipients limited to the group); `send_exchange_email` is the human confirmation; pre-approved reminders go out at their scheduled time with the saved text and are logged. Delivery status (queued, sent, delivered, bounced, complained, failed, suppressed) comes from the shared provider webhook; bounces and complaints join the suppression list; per-workspace and per-recipient daily limits apply. Workspace members get in-app and email notifications for new submissions, messages, uploaded documents and passed deadlines. The Access grid has **Send to people outside the workspace** (owners always; members when granted). Assistants read accepted submissions, messages and clarifications the user can read; unaccepted uploads are never readable by AI. Archiving an app stops its exchange links; restoring it re-enables the ones that have not expired. ## Continuity and UI

Failures retain the latest authorized usable published app and local input; run outcomes belong in Logs and private setup/history tools. Permission revocation still applies. Retries reconcile immutable receipts and expected record versions; never replace tables or overwrite newer user work to repair a task.

First-save prompts apply only to never-saved apps. Published apps stay usable during ChatGPT edits and later publication; updates may offer a subtle optional refresh notice. Keep Search and Add record inline, disclose secondary filters when space is tight, use bounded active-page reads and avoid redundant rendering after layout saves. Extra log filters stay inline and wrap naturally into another row; they never float over events. Sidebar order: Apps, Database, Access, Automations, Connectors, Logs. Templates are optional accelerators, not mandatory app types.

## Automation and connector editors

Web, ChatGPT and Claude use the same compact automation editor: title and Save/Discard in one top row, no Back to automations action, compact schedule/date controls, and icon-based worker choices. Database triggers cascade through **Table → Column → Change**, with Any column / Any change defaults for research and connector actions. Changing the table resets dependent choices; existing multi-column triggers and typed conditions remain intact until edited. Email permits entry creation or a column condition; omit Any change for email rather than showing a disabled choice. This restriction is worker-specific, not table-specific. If a broad trigger is selected before switching to Email, return to Trigger for an explicit supported selection. Keep card headings inside borders, including Trigger and Recipients.

Connectors always shows separate ChatGPT and Claude cards, including before setup. Status and dependent workflows come from the selected assistant's runner. A card is not proof of a connected account or an activated background runner. Start/continue setup in the appropriate assistant; another assistant cannot resume or edit its runner. Non-owners see the default cards with owner-managed status. Email is built in, not a connector.

The plugin and web share the connector configuration editor. Plugins can prepare configuration using authorized app/table/teammate choices. Final connector approval, secrets, credential replacement and external test requests retain the authenticated website boundary. Do not expose credential input or a model-side connector approval tool to achieve visual parity.

## Saving controls


Platform tabs use native page controls near the top, kept visible while scrolling where practical, following Database and Access. Do not add floating Save footers to ordinary platform or app editing pages. The exception is first publication of a new preview: the host shows a dark floating footer with Save app and Discard app, in both the website and ChatGPT extension.

Apps own their save behavior. Since session 4, record edits from an explicit action save directly (no review dialog); recommend saving tracker cell edits on completion, without a separate Save button, and native explicit Save/Ignore for layout and access. These are template recommendations, not platform-enforced UI. Custom apps can choose an appropriate interaction. First-time app publication uses that trusted preview approval footer; later app revisions retain native review controls. Preserve exact backend proposals, confirmation or authorized grants, optimistic versions, permissions and audit logs; a failed save retains the draft and supports safe retry. Once the server acknowledges a save, show its receipt separately from view refresh: retain the submitted view as read-only, keep a persistent reload-only retry on refresh failure, and never repeat an acknowledged mutation. Access loss removes retained content while keeping the receipt. Shared runtime, draft and layout hosts provide this automatically; selective refresh is still deferred.

## Assistant view context

The embedded workspace sends a minimal background hint through MCP Apps `ui/update-model-context` when the host advertises text context support: the current view or tab name, the app ID and the workspace ID, nothing else. App names and versions, routes, sections, tables, fields, selected records, scopes, editing state, record values, secrets and draft text are excluded; the assistant fetches further details through existing tools, which follow current permissions. The hint never opens or replaces the extension. Hidden cached views do not contribute context. Updates are debounced and serialized, replace earlier context, and do not trigger chat messages. The assistant must re-read data and permissions before acting. Hosts typically apply this context to the next conversation turn; unsupported hosts continue working without automatic view awareness. The website's assistant links pass only app and workspace IDs.

## Performance refresh boundaries

**Loading speed (22 November 2026).** The [loading speed scope](docs/scopes/LOADING_SPEED_2026-11-22.md) is built in four sessions; session L1 is built. Apps home and the archived list decide app access once per request with the lean app index (`src/workspace/app-index.ts`), which shares its rules with the per-app check and must stay equal to it (`tests/app-index.test.ts`). Home never reads app code; card miniatures are a later quiet read (`read_home_snapshots`); the archived count arrives with Home; query budgets are enforced by `tests/loading-speed.test.ts`. Access is never remembered between requests. Session L2 is built: opening an app (`readComponent` with its first screen, `runFrontendRequest` for frame opens) runs in one request-wide reuse scope, verified before release; its budget is in `tests/loading-speed-apps.test.ts`. App code is addressed by its fingerprint (`browser.have`, `read_app_code`, `src/shared/app-code.ts`); `open_app` gives the widget its first screen in result `_meta`. Session L3 is built: the template list carries a catalog fingerprint (`list_app_templates` with `catalog` answers `unchanged`), card images are 720×480 files kept by digest (`resources/template-gallery/cards/`, checked by `scripts/build-template-cards.mjs`), the widget loads them from the public `/template-cards/…` address or in one `read_app_template_cards` request, previews are prepared on hover or focus, and MCP requests answer `tools/list` from a prepared list and build only the called tool's group (`src/server/mcp-sections.ts`; handlers and authorization stay per request). Budgets in `tests/loading-speed-templates.test.ts` (list 1 query, widget file 1.9 MB). Session L4 is built: last known basics (`src/shared/last-known.ts`; names, kinds, dates, counts and tab names only, per person, cleared on sign-out or another person's sign-in, used only where storage is allowed) make return visits show Home, the archived count, templates and an app's frame at once, checked quietly. `baseApp` compresses responses (the `compression` middleware, never event streams). The plugin widget is two parts (`scripts/build-embed.ts`): part one is what `src/embed/main.tsx` reaches; part two (`src/embed/late-pages.tsx`) is `/widget-parts/<file>` or the widget-only `read_widget_part`, loaded by `src/embed/late-part.tsx` after the first screen. `HomePanel` takes its non-app screens from `HomeScreens` (`src/shared/home-screens.ts`). `TRIALY_WIDGET_SINGLE_FILE=1` builds one file. Claude's inline frame takes the widget's font (`WIDGET_FONT_SLOT`). Budgets in `tests/loading-speed-return.test.ts`; owner actions (capacity, real-host checklist) in `docs/scopes/LOADING_SPEED_L4_OWNER_ACTIONS.md`. The website's main script is split (L1.5): `src/web/main.tsx` holds the Apps page and an app's first screen; other signed-in pages come from `src/web/late-pages.ts` (loaded after `firstScreenShown()`, `src/shared/first-screen.ts`) and address pages from `src/web/route-pages.ts`, through `src/shared/lazy-page.tsx`. Never import a late page statically from `main.tsx`; shared code reaches Home's other screens and an app's access screen through `HomeScreens` and `appScreen()`. Budget in `tests/loading-speed-website.test.ts`.

Same-workspace/viewer metadata revalidation retains the editor through transient failures; authorization failure or scope changes clear it. Account reads after writes must bypass older pending snapshots, and old checks must not replace newer foreground results. Home idle revision checks cover metadata/access, not record samples or totals; returning/local changes refresh content. Revision aggregates are bounded in memory but still scan metadata, including relevant historical document versions. Keep P05 deploy/chunk recovery, P09's open tracker race, N1 no transaction held across sandbox execution and N3 no cancellation/replay of started actions explicit. CS2/DR2/IU2 are safety work. See [scope](docs/scopes/PLATFORM_AND_APP_PERFORMANCE_2026-11-13.md) and [measurement](docs/PERFORMANCE_MEASUREMENT.md).
